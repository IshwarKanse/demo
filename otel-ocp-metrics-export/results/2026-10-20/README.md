# Extended benchmark, 2026-10-20, OpenShift 5.0 on AWS, product collector 0.158.0

Cluster: OpenShift 5.0.0-0.nightly-2026-10-19-060022 on AWS, 3 control plane nodes and 4 workers
with 15.5 CPU and 58 GiB allocatable each, 244 pods running before the harness (254 with the user
workload monitoring stack and the harness, plus the load pods of each tier). Operator: Red Hat build
of OpenTelemetry 0.158.0-202610151241 from OperatorHub, collector image
registry.redhat.io/rhosdt/opentelemetry-collector-rhel9 (0.158.0) with the documented resilient
configuration (requests 500m/512Mi, limits 2 CPU/2Gi, memory_limiter, item-sized queue of 100,000
with block_on_overflow, exporter-side batching). Backend: the nop sink collector.

Matrix (`scripts/run-matrix.sh results/2026-10-20`, 20 minute windows after 5 minutes of
settling, one avalanche load pod = 25,000 gauge series scraped by user workload monitoring):
baseline without remote write, series tiers 0/4/10/20/40 load pods at a 30 s interval, rate tiers
at 10 load pods with 60 s and 15 s intervals, 20 load pods with 2 collector replicas, series churn
(4 load pods restarted every 10 minutes plus 4 pods whose series rotate every 10 minutes), a 150
minute window with a TSDB compaction inside, and a 5 minute backend outage at 8 load pods.

Raw series per run: `raw/<run>/<metric>.csv`; run metadata with the cluster shape and pod counts:
`runs.json`; aggregated table: `summary.md` (generated by `scripts/render-charts.py`); the
`collect.sh` rows: `table.md`. Charts in `charts/`.

## Findings

- Collector CPU is linear in the remote write rate and independent of the series count: 0.28 cores
  at 28k samples/s, 0.48 at 45k, 0.66 at 62k, 1.06 at 96k (one replica), about 1.1 cores per
  100,000 samples/s. The two rate tiers (same 250,000 extra series, 60 s and 15 s intervals) land
  on the same line as the series tiers.
- Collector memory is almost flat: the working set grows from 98 MiB to 151 MiB between 28k and 96k
  samples/s (1.5 million series per replica), RSS stays under 200 MiB. Two replicas at 62k
  samples/s used 0.70 cores and 231 MiB together, the same total as one replica.
- Platform Prometheus: remote write v2 costs a flat 0.1 CPU cores and about 1.4 GiB for both
  replicas (0.22 cores and 3.7 GiB without remote write, 0.29 to 0.38 cores and 5.1 to 5.6 GiB
  with it) and does not grow with the rate: the write-ahead log watcher and 4 shards per replica are
  the cost. The user workload Prometheus carries the load series: 0.35 cores and 7.1 GiB for both
  replicas at 1 million extra series.
- Series churn (about 2,200 new series/s in bursts, 100,000 series replaced per rollout) is paid
  by the user workload Prometheus (plus 1.2 GiB over 40 minutes until the next head truncation);
  the platform Prometheus, the collector and the remote write rate are unaffected.
- TSDB compaction: a one-minute CPU spike of the platform Prometheus from 0.38 to 0.5 cores, no
  memory change, no effect on the remote write rate or shard count, no collector effect.
- 5 minute backend outage at 40k samples/s: the queue filled to 100,000 items within a minute, the
  receiver refused the rest, Prometheus kept 1.3 million samples pending and retried; no collector
  restart, no failed sample. After the sink came back the backlog drained in 245 s at up to 125k
  points/s, with the collector at its 2 core limit and a 2 GiB working-set peak under the
  memory_limiter.

Earlier runs (2026-10-18, smaller cluster) are in the sibling files of this directory's parent.
