## Sizing

Measured from the vendor stand-in Prometheus only (cAdvisor container metrics of the collector pods and the
collectors' own telemetry, both scraped by the cluster collector), `scripts/run-sizing.sh` / `scripts/collect.sh`.

Backend matters for the measurement: with the vendor stand-in Prometheus as the backend the cluster collector reported
about 600 to 6,000 refused points/s on the memory_limiter and receiver counters, although the memory limiter never
logged a soft limit crossing. The counters count the scrapes whose export blocked on the full sending queue until the
scrape timeout: the stand-in Prometheus cannot ingest about 450,000 series fast enough. As in the option 1 benchmark,
the sizing runs therefore export the platform metrics to the nop sink collector (`BACKEND=sink` variant of the
generator), and only the collectors' own telemetry goes to the vendor Prometheus. Against the sink the refused
counters are 0. Note that the prometheus receiver counts a histogram or summary as one data point, so the accepted
points/s are far below the series count divided by the scrape interval.

### Sizing summary (steady state, nop sink as backend, 6 nodes)

| Pods in the cluster | Node collector per pod: CPU cores / RSS MiB / points/s (all 6) | Cluster collector: CPU cores / RSS MiB / points/s |
|---|---|---|
| 296 | 0.002 / 140 / 442 | 0.09 / 1,050 / 6,030 |
| 596 (+300 idle) | 0.002 / 142 / 752 | 0.12 / 1,300 / 9,130 |
| 896 (+600 idle) | 0.003 / 149 / 1,062 | 0.17 / 1,810 / 12,230 |

The prometheus receiver counts a histogram or a summary as one data point, so the points/s of the cluster collector are
far below its series count (about 458,000 series at 296 pods) divided by the 30 s interval. The cluster collector's
memory grows with the number of series it holds in the scrape state: about 1 GiB for the control plane and the
operators of a six-node cluster plus about 1.3 MiB per additional pod (cAdvisor and kubelet_stats series); its CPU stays
below 0.2 cores. The node collectors are small: about 150 MiB RSS and a few millicores per node at 300 pods per node.

Recommendation (product collector 0.158.0): node collector requests 50m / 192Mi, limits 500m / 512Mi; cluster collector
requests 500m / 1Gi, limits 2 CPU / 4Gi for clusters up to about 1,000 pods; beyond that add about 1.5 GiB of memory
limit per 1,000 pods, drop the kubelet and cAdvisor node-proxy jobs when the kubelet_stats data suffices (the cAdvisor
job alone is 110,000 series on this cluster), or keep only the API server metrics that are needed. The item-sized
sending queue must exceed the largest single scrape (200,000 items for the cluster collector, 50,000 for the node
collector), otherwise the exporter rejects the scrape with `element size too large`.

### Backend outage of 5 min (sink scaled to 0 from 2026-10-19T07:00:20Z, window incl. 5 min recovery)
| collector | restarts | max queue (items) | receiver refused points | exporter failed points | receiver accepted points | exporter sent points | max RSS MiB | queue drained after (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| otel-node | 0 | 28049 | 0 | - | 267568 | 271921 | 155 | 90 |
| otel-cluster | 0 | 199996 (full) | 0 | - | 2323103 | 2325126 | 1395 | 30 |

The node collectors (442 points/s together, queue 50,000 items each) kept scraping through the whole outage: the queues
peaked at 28,000 items, nothing was refused or lost, and they drained 90 s after the sink came back. The cluster
collector (6,000 points/s) filled its 200,000-item queue after about 60 s; with `block_on_overflow: true` the scrape
pipeline then blocked, the accepted rate fell to about 10 points/s (the k8s_cluster and telemetry trickle) for the
remaining 4 minutes, and those control plane scrapes are the data that is lost: the prometheus receiver has no
upstream WAL, a blocked scrape is a skipped scrape. After the sink came back the backlog drained in 30 s at 22,000
points/s with RSS peaking at 1.4 GiB (steady state 1.0 to 1.1 GiB: about 0.4 KiB of RSS per queued point). No restart,
no refused or failed points on either collector. To survive a longer outage without loss, size the queue for it (the
cluster collector of this cluster needs about 6,000 items per second of outage, about 2.4 MiB of memory per second) or
add the `file_storage` extension to the sending queue; with 200,000 items the documented configuration bridges about
30 seconds for the control plane scrapes and over 10 minutes for the node collectors.

### Backend outage of 5 min, file_storage queue (sink scaled to 0 from 2026-10-19T07:46:53Z, window incl. 5 min recovery)
| collector | restarts | max queue (items) | receiver refused points | exporter failed points | receiver accepted points | exporter sent points | max RSS MiB | queue drained after (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| otel-node | 0 | 27927 | 0 | - | 267635 | 267635 | 153 | 60 |
| otel-cluster | 0 | 200000 (full) | 0 | - | 2286221 | 2450353 | 1187 | 30 |

With the `file_storage` extension on the cluster collector's sending queue (`FILE_STORAGE=1` variant: emptyDir at
`/var/lib/otelcol/file_storage`, `sending_queue.storage: file_storage`) the outage behaved the same way: the queue
filled its 200,000 items after about 60 s, the scrapes blocked for the remaining 4 minutes, and the backlog drained in
30 s after the sink came back. The difference is where the queue lives: RSS peaked at 1.2 GiB instead of 1.4 GiB and
stayed at about 1.09 GiB during the outage. What the persistent queue adds is that its content survives a collector
restart (not exercised here) and that a much larger `queue_size` is affordable because the items are on disk; to
survive a long outage without skipped scrapes, raise `queue_size` together with `file_storage` and give the collector a
persistent volume so that the queue also survives a pod reschedule.
